Mentor Terms of Service

The deal between you and Batch Zero when you mentor a team. Last updated September 25, 2026.

A plain-English draft written at the pre-incorporation stage — not legal advice, and worth a lawyer's pass once Batch Zero has paid mentors or larger cohorts.

1. Your role

As a Batch Zero mentor you agree to advise founders in your assigned cohort as a volunteer — usually about 1–2 hours a week: one session with a team, plus light prep. Unless we agree otherwise in writing, mentoring is unpaid.

2. Use of your profile

You agree that Batch Zero may show your name, photo, title, company and short bio on the Batch Zero website and in related material — the mentor roster, cohort announcements, social posts — to introduce you to founders and the community. Ask us to change or remove any of it at any time at hello@batchzero.co.

3. Confidentiality

Founders share early, non-public details of their startups with you. Keep that to yourself, and don't use it outside advising them.

4. No ownership of founder work

Advice, feedback or introductions you give don't give you ownership, equity or intellectual property in a founder's company or work, unless a separate written agreement says so.

5. Working with students

Founders in the program are high-school students, most of them under 18. Sessions happen on the platform or on recorded calls, never in private one-to-one channels, and you agree to follow the mentor code of conduct, which forms part of these terms. Mentors are interviewed and background-checked before being matched.

6. Conduct

Engage with founders professionally and respectfully, and represent Batch Zero fairly when you're acting as a mentor. Breaching the code of conduct ends your involvement immediately.

7. No liability

You're advising in good faith as a volunteer. Neither you nor Batch Zero is liable for what founders do with that advice, and the program is provided as is, without warranties.

8. Ending it

You or Batch Zero can end your involvement at any time, for any reason, by telling the other.

9. Governing law

These terms are governed by the laws of the State of California.
